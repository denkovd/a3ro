# Macro Chaos Engine: thesis review and module proposal

Reviewed 16 October 2026. Proposed outlook period: 2026–2046.

## Assessment

The central question is useful: how do productivity, physical infrastructure, fiscal policy and ownership interact over the next twenty years? The strongest part is the connection between technological adoption and the infrastructure required to support it. The weakest part is presenting one sequence of events, one energy technology and one group of investments as inevitable.

Build this as an outlook with competing scenarios and evidence that can change the view. The original essay can be an attributed thesis inside that module, after factual corrections. It is not yet a forecasting model or a tested allocation strategy.

This review checks claims against primary sources, performs numerical consistency checks, examines historical counterexamples and reviews the existing A3RO macro structure. It does not establish predictive accuracy or report a portfolio backtest. The proposal provides no reproducible entry, exit, weighting or rebalancing rules from which to calculate one.

## Claim audit

### 1. AI increases the importance of electricity infrastructure — supported, with limits

The IEA's 2026 central projection puts data-centre electricity demand at approximately 485 TWh in 2025 and 950 TWh in 2030, about 3% of global electricity demand then. Its analysis identifies constraints in grid connections, equipment, chips and financing. Those findings support regional infrastructure bottlenecks; they do not establish a global hydrocarbon exhaustion date. The figures cover all data centres, not AI alone. [IEA, Key Questions on Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary).

The analytical model should separate workload growth, energy per workload, geographic concentration and available generation. Exponential growth in compute does not automatically imply the same growth in energy: efficiency and changes in workload mix intervene. Electricity demand is also not interchangeable with oil consumption.

### 2. Technology makes everything cheaper and forces matching money creation — overstated

Technology can reduce unit costs. Whether consumers receive those savings depends on competition, adoption, fixed costs, market power and complementary inputs. Cheaper digital services can coexist with expensive land, healthcare, labour or electricity.

There is no demonstrated rule that central banks create an equal or greater quantity of currency for every productivity gain. The essay conflates fiscal spending, central-bank reserves, commercial-bank deposits and asset valuations. Commercial-bank lending is a major source of money creation, while monetary policy commonly operates through interest rates. [Bank of England, Money creation in the modern economy](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy).

Replace the asserted mechanical relationship with a hypothesis about how policy responds to productivity, employment and inflation. Measure those responses separately.

### 3. The 2% target exists to service unpayable debt — incorrect as stated

The Federal Reserve formally adopted its numerical 2% objective in 2012. Its stated rationale concerns price stability and employment; modest positive inflation also leaves more room to reduce nominal rates during downturns. Describing this as an early-twentieth-century target is inaccurate. [Federal Reserve, policy history](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20231010a.htm); [Federal Reserve, historical approaches](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/historical-approaches-to-monetary-policy.htm).

Unexpected inflation can reduce the real value of existing fixed nominal debt. However, refinancing at higher yields, indexed spending and debt maturity can offset that benefit. Fiscal pressure can influence policy without making monetary accommodation inevitable.

The fiscal concern itself has evidence: CBO's February 2026 current-law baseline projects US public debt rising from 101% of GDP in 2026 to 120% in 2036. That is a conditional projection of pressure, not a dated prediction of collapse. [CBO, Budget and Economic Outlook](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105).

### 4. A 50-year fuel runway becomes a 15–20-year wall — unsupported timing

A reserves-to-production ratio divides reserves by current annual production or consumption; it does not incorporate a growth forecast. Proved reserves also change with prices, technology and development. The EIA explicitly warns against treating that ratio as a depletion countdown. [EIA, future oil availability](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=38&t=6).

An economic supply constraint can occur before physical depletion. Establishing its date still requires explicit assumptions about field decline, investment, substitution, demand, costs and prices. None are provided here. Keep energy-security and affordability risks; remove the countdown until a model supports it.

### 5. Falling EROI matters — supported mechanism, unverified universal numbers

Energy return on investment depends on the fuel, location, period and accounting boundary. A historical wellhead ratio cannot be compared directly with a modern delivered-energy ratio. Brockway and colleagues demonstrate how primary-stage and final-stage estimates differ materially; their study covers 1995–2011 and is not a measurement of the current global ratio. [Brockway et al., Nature Energy, 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-019-0425-z).

The claimed universal progression from 100:1 to 10:1 needs matched evidence. EROI measures energy, not financial cost, and does not by itself determine either an oil price or a collapse date.

### 6. Cheap, rapidly deployed SMRs are the only solution — not established

SMRs merit a scenario because firm power can be valuable. Manufacturing repeatability, construction time, financing, fuel availability and completed-project costs remain variables to test. The IEA's nuclear outlook makes widespread competitive deployment conditional on cost reductions and enabling investment. [IEA, The Path to a New Era for Nuclear Energy](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-path-to-a-new-era-for-nuclear-energy/executive-summary).

The blanket dismissal of renewables conflicts with Australia's latest GenCost findings: the July 2026 final report finds renewables supported by storage provide the lowest-cost pathway to net zero for Australia's electricity system. That result is specific to its geography and assumptions; it does not settle every power market. [CSIRO, GenCost 2025–26](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/all/news/2026/july/gencost-2025-26).

Compare actual delivered power with equivalent reliability and connection dates, incorporating storage, firming, transmission, financing and construction risk. Standalone LCOE is insufficient to decide which project supplies a continuous data-centre load economically.

### 7. Copper, lithium and scarce assets must appreciate — directionally plausible demand, invalid investment shortcut

Mineral demand does not establish investment returns. Supply additions, substitution, recycling, operating costs, taxes, financing and the price paid for the asset all matter. Surface land, extraction rights, an operating mine, a commodity and a mining share are different exposures.

The IEA's 2026 outlook estimates a potential copper supply gap of 25% in 2035 against its project pipeline, narrowed from approximately 30% in the previous outlook. The changing estimate illustrates why a projected gap must be updated rather than treated as destiny. [IEA, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2026/executive-summary).

A historical counterexample is lithium: the IEA's 2025 report recorded a decline exceeding 80% from 2023 despite the longer-term electrification story. The 2026 report describes a subsequent recovery. A correct structural demand thesis can coexist with a severe investment drawdown. [IEA, 2025 minerals outlook](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2025/executive-summary).

### 8. Housing at 20 times income proves monetary failure — incomplete measurement and causality

Specify geography, date, dwelling type, household versus individual income, and median versus mean before using the multiple. A multi-standard-deviation claim additionally requires a reference distribution. Housing is also illiquid, despite being financeable and usable as collateral.

Interest rates matter, but housing supply, population, lending conditions and expectations also matter. RBA research finds different price responses to monetary policy across local housing markets, with some evidence that land supply helps explain the differences. [RBA, Distributional Effects of Monetary Policy](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/2020/2020-02/full.html).

Economic frustration can explain speculative behaviour without establishing that a leveraged bet has a favourable expected return. Avoid language that turns an explanation of desperation into an endorsement of leverage.

### 9. A million-dollar Bitcoin follows mathematically from debasement — false without additional assumptions

Scarcity constrains supply; it does not guarantee demand or real purchasing power. A nominal price target needs a starting price, date, currency and assumptions about real demand. The arithmetic below shows the gap in the argument.

The proposed inflation bucket also fails as an unconditional short-horizon hedge: Bitcoin lost 64.2% in 2022 while US CPI rose 6.5% December to December. This counterexample rejects an automatic inflation-to-return mapping; it does not disprove every longer-term Bitcoin thesis. [CoinGecko, 2022 Annual Report](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/2022-annual-crypto-report); [BLS, December 2022 CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_01122023.htm).

### 10. Quantum computing is the primary Bitcoin risk — incomplete ranking

Quantum risk is relevant, but the essay supplies no basis for ranking it above demand, regulation, custody, leverage, market structure or network-economics risks. The relevant threshold is a cryptographically capable quantum computer, not merely a demonstration of quantum advantage.

The signature discussion should cover ECDSA and Schnorr. Migration proposals exist, but proposals are not deployed protection or a guarantee that every holder migrates. BIP 360 remains marked Draft; BIP 361 discusses migration and legacy signatures. [Bitcoin BIP 360](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0360.mediawiki); [Bitcoin BIP 361](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0361.mediawiki).

### 11. A Fourth Turning dates the reset — narrative framing, not a tested clock

No operational definition, validated dating procedure or predictive test is supplied. If a twenty-year phase begins in 2020, it ends around 2040. A new twenty-year outlook beginning in October 2026 ends in 2046. Those are different horizons.

If retained, identify the generational-cycle argument as an interpretive framework. Define observable outcomes and dates before scoring it. Terms such as reset, endgame and collapse cannot serve as measured outcomes until their meanings are specified.

## Numerical checks performed

### Inflation and a nominal Bitcoin target

Assume an illustrative USD 100,000 starting value, constant real value and annual price inflation compounded for twenty years. This is not a current Bitcoin quote or a forecast.

Formula: nominal future value = starting value × (1 + annual inflation)^20.

| Annual inflation | Price-level multiplier | Nominal value preserving the initial real value | Purchasing power remaining in non-interest-bearing cash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2% | 1.486× | $148,595 | 67.3% |
| 3% | 1.806× | $180,611 | 55.4% |
| 5% | 2.653× | $265,330 | 37.7% |
| 10% | 6.727× | $672,750 | 14.9% |

A tenfold nominal increase over twenty years requires 12.20% annual growth. Inflation alone would need that rate under the constant-real-value assumption. Lower inflation requires real appreciation as well. Consumer-price inflation, money-supply growth and currency depreciation are different quantities and should not be substituted for each other.

Interest-bearing saving is a separate calculation: real return = (1 + nominal return) / (1 + inflation) − 1, before tax and fees. The cash column does not describe every savings account or short-term government instrument.

### EROI consistency

For a consistently defined energy boundary, the net share of gross energy is 1 − 1 / EROI.

| EROI | Share used as energy input | Net share |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100:1 | 1% | 99% |
| 10:1 | 10% | 90% |
| 5:1 | 20% | 80% |
| 3:1 | 33.3% | 66.7% |

The change from 100:1 to 10:1 is consequential, but it is not a 90% loss of net energy at unchanged gross output. This accounting identity cannot establish the actual global EROI trend or the timing of a shortage.

## Suggested 2026–2046 structure

Use the following as planning windows, not predictions that events must happen on schedule.

| Window | Main questions | Evidence that should change the view |
|---|---|---|
| 2026–2030: construction and adoption | Can grids and equipment suppliers serve new loads? Does AI spending produce paid demand and measurable productivity? | Energised capacity versus announcements; connection times; equipment deliveries; utilisation; revenue and cash flow; productivity data |
| 2031–2036: diffusion and adjustment | Do productivity gains spread beyond technology firms? Which power technologies deliver repeatable economics? How do governments manage financing and demographic costs? | Broad productivity and real-wage gains; completed-project costs; debt-service burden; adoption outside early users |
| 2037–2046: alternative outcomes | Does cheaper production outweigh new demand and scarcity? Who receives the gains? Do institutions adapt? | Delivered energy costs; purchasing power; labour income; housing access; fiscal stability; ownership concentration |

Four scenarios should remain visible. They are qualitative alternatives, not calibrated probabilities, and regions can follow different paths.

| Scenario | Conditions | Main implication to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Broad productivity gains | Technology diffuses, power supply expands, fiscal institutions retain credibility | More output and stronger real incomes; scarcity premiums may decline even when usage grows |
| Persistent bottlenecks | Infrastructure and materials lag demand; financing and trade barriers slow expansion | Regional price spikes and uneven returns; some suppliers gain while customers and overleveraged builders suffer |
| Fiscal and monetary stress | Debt service and deficits overwhelm adjustment; confidence in policy weakens | Higher inflation or currency pressure becomes possible; outcomes depend on currency, policy and starting valuations |
| Investment bust and slow diffusion | AI revenues disappoint, utilisation falls, credit tightens | Equipment oversupply, defaults or write-downs can coexist with long-term technological progress |

The original essay is closest to the second and third scenarios, with an assumed eventual nuclear solution. It needs credible versions of the first and fourth to function as a decision tool.

For a broad twenty-year outlook, also include demographics and pensions, labour displacement and distribution, geopolitics and trade, climate and adaptation, and institutional responses. These are missing drivers to model, not additional predictions established by this review.

## Module design for A3RO

Suggested name: **Macro Horizon — 2026–2046**. The original title can label the author's thesis, but it preselects an outcome when used as the title of the entire analytical module.

The existing macro brief uses 0–3M, 3–12M and 12M+ horizons in `app/components/projects/macro/macroBrief.ts`. The regime engine in `backend/src/macro/engine.ts` classifies acceleration and deceleration of growth and inflation. Its DEFLATION label does not necessarily mean that the general price level is falling. Keep this distinction explicit in the new module.

Use a separate structural outlook with links to the tactical regime and commodity modules. A short-term risk-off signal can coexist with a constructive twenty-year view. Existing tactical results cannot validate a new structural allocation rule.

Recommended initial contents:

1. A dated outlook covering the three planning windows and four scenarios.
2. A thesis register with evidence, counterevidence and an explicit condition for revision.
3. A compact dashboard showing observed trends separately from source projections and editorial interpretations.
4. A scenario comparison of possible asset sensitivities, including valuation and failure conditions.
5. An update history that preserves what was previously predicted and why it changed.

Each thesis should store a claim, geography, horizon, source publication date, observation date, units, nominal/real basis, evidence status and review condition. Missing data should remain missing. Several correlated indicators should not be counted as independent confirmations, and an arbitrary composite score should not be labelled a probability.

Candidate monitoring areas:

| Area | Indicators | Evidence against the original thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Productivity | Output per hour, adoption, unit labour costs, real wages | Broad real-income gains without persistent inflation acceleration |
| Fiscal pressure | Primary balance, interest/revenue, maturity profile, effective interest versus nominal GDP growth | Improving debt dynamics and credible fiscal adjustment |
| Monetary conditions | Policy rates, real yields, bank credit, broad money, inflation expectations | Restrictive policy and stable expectations despite technological progress |
| Electricity | Actual load, energised generation, connections, congestion, delivered prices | More reliable supply and falling real delivered costs |
| SMR delivery | Construction and commissioning, completed costs, repeat orders | Repeated delays or uneconomic completed projects |
| Materials | Mine output, inventories, projects, substitution and recycling | Supply growth and substitution repeatedly exceeding demand |
| Housing | Local price/income, rents, completions, household formation, debt service | Affordability improves through supply and income growth |
| Bitcoin | Adoption, liquidity, real returns, drawdowns, security and migration progress | Real demand weakens or the asset persistently fails the defined hedge test |

Review slow structural indicators when new releases arrive, with an editorial synthesis each quarter. Fast market prices can refresh more often without implying the twenty-year outlook has equal precision.

## What a genuine allocation test would require

Before testing the proposed matrix, define assets, weights, signal inputs, decision dates, release lags, rebalancing, leverage and costs. Distinguish falling inflation from falling prices, and distinguish demand destruction from productivity-led disinflation. Long-duration bonds also depend on real yields, term premiums and credit/currency conditions; compute infrastructure is not automatically defensive in a recession.

Use point-in-time data and rolling out-of-sample evaluation. Include several inflation, disinflation and credit episodes, and compare against simple investable benchmarks. Report real and nominal total returns, drawdowns, turnover and the sensitivity to alternative signal definitions. Handle assets with short histories explicitly. Do not fill pre-Bitcoin periods with invented returns or present a fitted historical regime as information available to investors at the time.

The near-term test should be a dated prediction log: measurable targets and ranges, preserved vintages, scheduled reviews and recorded misses. That provides evidence about the module's usefulness before twenty years have elapsed.

## Suggested replacement premise

Over the next twenty years, AI and automation could expand productive capacity while electricity networks, materials, housing and institutions adjust at different speeds. Fiscal burdens and demographic change will influence how the gains and costs are distributed. The resulting path could include periods of disinflation, supply shocks, investment excess and policy stress. This module tracks the evidence for those competing outcomes, the conditions that would change the outlook and the distinction between a growing industry and an attractive investment.
